01

The Purple Cow structure.

Blake's framework as Matheus and Gabi wrote it up. A rare thing never seen before, that moves you to share it immediately. Four questions gate every concept; five blocks carry every caption.

Four questions, asked before anything gets made

  1. Does this make you want to stop everything you are doing to look at it?
  2. Would it make somebody stop and verbally say “that's good”?
  3. Would this move someone immediately to share?
  4. Does it hit your heart or your brain in a new place where it never has been hit before?

Where the cow is, for this brand

The peptide category advertises adjectives. Premium. Uncompromising. 99%+ pure, set large over a mountain. Almost nobody publishes anything a buyer can actually check.

So the cow is the paperwork. We put the lab report in the ad. We publish the milligrams the lab measured beside the milligrams we printed — including the lot that came in under label. In a category built on adjectives, an audited number is the rare thing.

03

What we can and cannot say.

Blake's note was to be careful with the verbiage. This is the working version of that, drawn from the compliance rules already governing the site and the blog. The audience for every ad is a qualified researcher — never a consumer, a patient, or an athlete.

Fair game SAY

  • Purity measured by HPLC, quoted per lot with the lab named“98.76%, ILS Laboratories” — not “99%+ pure”
  • Measured net content against labeled content, and fill accuracyThe category's blind spot. Use it constantly.
  • Batch-specific Certificate of Analysis, tied to the lot number on the vial
  • Independent third-party testing, with the lab namedILS Laboratories, Janoshik, Testides, Kovera Labs
  • Lyophilized powder, vial size, storage and handling as bench procedure
  • Reconstitution math as a concentration that describes the vialmg/mL in the vial. Never a volume to administer.
  • Shipping facts: cold-handled, plain unbranded packaging, tracked, most orders out within one business day
  • Prices, sizes, STEADFAST10 for 10% off a first order, free shipping over $200, Subscribe & Save 5%
  • “For laboratory and research use only. Not for human consumption.”

Never, in any channel DON'T

  • Any benefit, result, or outcomeweight, fat, muscle, recovery, healing, sleep, energy, anti-aging, performance
  • Any therapeutic framingtreat, cure, prevent, heal, therapy, remedy, reverses
  • Dosing of any kindamounts, schedules, cycles, stacks, protocols, titration, mg/kg as a dose
  • Second person aimed at an effectyour results, you'll notice, what to expect, feel the effects
  • Consumer or patient framingpatients, consumers, your body, your health, talk to your doctor, supplement
  • “Safe”, “safe for humans”, “pharmaceutical grade”, or any implication of approval
  • Comparisons to a prescription drug's effects, or brand names like Ozempic / Mounjaro / Zepbound
  • Before-and-after imagery, bodies, syringes, needles, injection scenes
  • Testimonials or reviews that describe an effect
  • Urgency theatrea real code and a real threshold are fine; invented countdowns are not
Mandatory footer strip — every creative, every size, one line, never below 17px at 1080

For laboratory and research use only. Not for human consumption.

The long-form disclaimer (“not a drug and not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease”) appears in every caption instead — the artwork carries the label wording, the caption carries the full block.

06

Production specs.

Sizes & safe areas

Feed 1080×1440 portrait — it takes more of the screen on the way past than a square does. Story 1080×1920 with 250px clear at top and bottom for the profile bar and the tap target. Export PNG for anything carrying a chromatogram or a document, JPG q90 for vial-only art.

The footer strip is not optional and does not scale below 15px at 1080. It lives inside the artwork rather than the caption, because Meta truncates captions and never crops pixels.

Type & colour

Space Grotesk 600–700 for headlines, Inter 300 for decks, Space Mono for anything that reads as data: lot numbers, purity figures, URLs, promo codes.

Three grounds, all from the brand bible: Carbon #0D0D0D, Field #EFEDE6, Olive #414B37, accent olive #8A9A6B. Vial renders only ever sit on a light panel, so the white studio background knocks out cleanly on multiply.

Regenerating the files

Copy and artwork both live in pack.js; the creative CSS lives in creative.css. Edit either and both this page and the exports change together.

To re-export: node export.mjs from the ads/ folder. It drives headless Chrome over export.html?id=F01 and writes a PNG and a PDF per asset into exports/.

Changing a creative does not update its Canva design — Canva holds a copy taken at import time. Re-import the new PDF and swap the id in the CANVA map in pack.js.

Before anything goes live

Check the caption against section 03 line by line, then check every figure against the proof bank in section 02 — lab name, lot, and number together, or the figure comes out.

Landing page per concept: proof concepts go to /coa, product concepts to the PDP, education concepts to the blog post. Never point a proof ad at the cart.

  • Two concepts need a photograph we don't have. F08 wants the actual plain shipping box, F09 wants a tight crop of a real printed label. Both currently render with stand-ins.
  • Decide the channel for S04 before it ships. It names the contaminant screen in the caption and is marked organic-only for that reason.
